When regimes cycle

Why countries keep swinging between democracy and autocracy, and what starts and stops the swings.

The question

Many countries swing between democracy and autocracy for decades. Polybius called the pattern anacyclosis. Do the swings need a run of bad luck, or do they have an engine of their own? In this model they have one, and the economy decides when it starts and when it stops.

The model

A country is two numbers, measured around its steady state: inclusiveness xxx, how open its institutions are, and mobilization mmm, how many of its people are mobilized. Mobilization has a ceiling KKK: the pool of young people free to be mobilized. Institutions absorb activists, and hhh is the time they need per activist. The structure is the Rosenzweig and MacArthur model from population ecology.

Inclusive institutions exist only if v>cηv > c\etav>cη: an elite's gain from absorbing an opponent, vvv, must exceed the cost of keeping institutions open, cηc\etacη. Otherwise x→0x \to 0x→0 from every starting point.

What moves a country across

Falling behind the neighbours enlarges the pool of young people and raises KKK. A crisis speeds the erosion of institutions, which drops the country below the threshold and closes the regime. New autocracies also respond to an outside pull toward reopening, which acts only until they consolidate.

The evidence

The tests use V-Dem from 1950 to 2019 and repeat under eight codings of regime type. All three predictions hold:

  1. Young autocracies that fall behind their neighbours open up.
  2. Crises keep autocracies shut and break young democracies.
  3. New autocracies often fall when a neighbour turns democratic, until they consolidate.

What it means

The cycle is conditional. The economy sets which side of K∗K^*K∗ a country sits on, and one pool of young people answers two triggers: relative decline opens autocracies, outright crisis breaks young democracies. The thresholds also say what any shock must move to matter: vvv against cηc\etacη, or hhh and with it K∗K^*K∗.
