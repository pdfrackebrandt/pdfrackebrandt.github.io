Reversed by our own replication

Pedro F. Rackebrandt

Why does this matter? An automated auditor that checks research extractions is right only about a third of the time, yet a wrong extraction can still return a theorem. We pre-registered three signals that might catch the error, then tested them once on frontier models. Two reversed. Cross-model disagreement is the one that held.

On 174 items, a model disagreeing with its own repeats cleared the bar in only one family. Disagreement across models reached 0.75 and stayed above the threshold. It flagged errors better on unconventional designs. Abstaining where models disagree most trims the error rate from 81% to 72%, but the tool remains weak.

How to read the figure

Each row is a registered comparison. The open square is the discovery sample on five open-weight arms. The filled circle is the confirmatory run on four frontier families. The dashed navy line is the 0.65 threshold we deposited before seeing those families. The dotted line is chance.

Task structure falls from 0.771 to about 0.50 and is retracted. Within-model sampling falls from 0.727 and clears the bar in one family of four. Cross-model disagreement rises from 0.609 to 0.753 and is held.
