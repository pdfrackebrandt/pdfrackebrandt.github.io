Matrix algebra review

Rank, inverse, quadratic forms, and the matrix calculus used in the OLS first-order conditions.

All notes

1. Basic operations

A symmetric matrix equals its transpose. The identity, diagonal matrices, and the vector of ones appear constantly. Addition is entrywise; multiplication is not commutative.

Takeaway

X'X is symmetric and positive semidefinite. It is invertible exactly when X has full column rank.

2. Rank, inverse and determinants

Full column rank means the columns are linearly independent. The determinant of a 2×2 matrix is the signed area of the parallelogram spanned by its columns. A singular matrix has determinant zero and no inverse.

3. Quadratic forms and derivatives

The quadratic form β'Aβ is positive for all β ≠ 0 when A is positive definite. The gradient of β'X'Xβ with respect to β is 2X'Xβ. These two facts give the OLS first-order condition.
