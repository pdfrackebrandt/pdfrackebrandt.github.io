OLS estimator and finite-sample properties

How the OLS estimator is derived, when it is unbiased, what its variance is, and why it is the best linear unbiased estimator.

All notes

1. The linear model and the OLS estimator

The model is y = Xβ + u. Linear means linear in the parameters β. The regressors can be any known functions of the data. Stacking the observations, y is n×1, X is n×k, β is k×1, and u is n×1. We observe y and X; β is fixed and unknown; u is unobserved.

OLS chooses β to minimise the sum of squared residuals. The solution is β̂ = (X'X)^{-1} X'y when X has full column rank. The residuals are orthogonal to every column of X: X'û = 0. Fitted values are the projection of y onto the column space of X.

Takeaway

Any model that is linear in the parameters fits the OLS framework, however nonlinear the relationship between y and the raw data.

2. Finite-sample properties

Under OLS.1–3 (linearity, full column rank, strict exogeneity E[u|X]=0) the estimator is unbiased: E[β̂|X] = β. Under OLS.1–4 (plus spherical errors: homoskedasticity and no serial correlation) the conditional variance is σ²(X'X)^{-1}.

The Gauss–Markov theorem says that under these four assumptions OLS is the best linear unbiased estimator: among all linear unbiased estimators it has the smallest variance. The proof uses the fact that any other linear unbiased estimator can be written as OLS plus a term whose variance is positive.

Note

Strict exogeneity is stronger than zero correlation. It is what delivers unbiasedness at every sample size.

3. Residual maker and fitted values

The projection matrix P = X(X'X)^{-1}X' is symmetric and idempotent. Fitted values are ŷ = Py. The residual maker M = I − P is also symmetric and idempotent. Residuals are û = My. M annihilates any linear combination of the columns of X, so the residuals are orthogonal to the regressors.

Takeaway

OLS is the orthogonal projection of y onto the column space of X. The residual is the shortest vector from y to that subspace.
