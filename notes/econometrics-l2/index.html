Estimation, asymptotics and confidence intervals

LLN (weak and strong), CLT, Slutsky, consistency versus unbiasedness, and asymptotic intervals.

All notes

1. Estimators and the analogy principle

An estimator is a function of the random sample, hence a random variable. An estimate is the value it takes in one sample. Population quantities are replaced by sample analogues: expectations become averages, the CDF becomes the EDF.

Takeaway

An estimator is a random variable. Asymptotics describe its distribution as n grows.

2. Law of large numbers

The weak LLN and strong LLN both require the same moment conditions. The strong version gives almost-sure convergence. Cauchy has no mean, so averaging fails.

3. Central limit theorem and Slutsky

The CLT says the standardised sample mean is approximately normal. Slutsky lets you replace consistent estimators inside continuous functions without changing the limiting distribution.

4. Consistency versus unbiasedness

Unbiasedness is a finite-sample property. Consistency is asymptotic: the estimator converges in probability to the true value. An estimator can be biased yet consistent, or unbiased yet inconsistent.
